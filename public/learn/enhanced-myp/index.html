What actually changes — in 90 seconds

The Enhanced MYP is an evolution, not a rewrite. The IB kept the soul of the programme (concept-driven inquiry, international-mindedness) and simplified the paperwork around it.

🧭

Less bureaucracy

Shorter ATL lists. No more rigid "In order to… students must…" statement. The planner is a thinking tool, not a compliance form.

🎯

More teacher agency

You decide when and how to teach a skill. The terms implicit and explicit are gone — teach concepts the way your learners need them.

🔍

Sharper inquiry

The Statement of Inquiry becomes an Inquiry Question a 12-year-old actually wants to answer. Curiosity over compliance.

🤝

With, not for

"Service as Action" becomes Community Engagement. Action with the community, not to it. Dialogue, relationships, principled action.

📐

Three criteria. Period.

Every subject now uses 3 criteria with fewer strands. Design slims 16 strands to 6. Arts, PHE and Design share a family.

🌍

Lens it

Pair any Global Context with one of three Contextual Lenses: Individual · Local · Global. No more generic tagging.

Read this if you're brand new to the MYP

The Middle Years Programme is the IB's framework for ages 11–16 (Y1–Y5). Students learn through inquiry, in eight subject groups, anchored by big concepts and real-world contexts. They complete a Community Project (Y3) and a Personal Project (Y5). The Enhanced MYP changes how teachers plan and assess — not what students learn.

Deep dive

The four pillars of change

Tap a tab to explore each pillar. Each section has a "What changed", "Why it matters", and a "Try this Monday" prompt.

1. Specified Concepts

What changed: The two-tier "Key + Related" concept system is gone. Each subject group now has a single curated list of about 25+ Specified Concepts. Schools can add their own (e.g. justice, sustainability).

Why it matters: Concepts become functional — they should appear in the inquiry question, the teaching, and the summative task. Anything else is decoration.

Try this Monday: Open one current unit. Highlight the Key Concept. Ask: "If I removed this, would the unit still work?" If yes — it was decoration. Replace it with one that the assessment task actually rewards.

Plain-English definition

A specified concept is a big idea your subject keeps returning to — the thing students will still understand five years from now, when they've forgotten the textbook page.

Watch out for…

  • Concepts that sound impressive but don't drive the assessment.
  • Adding "school concepts" without retiring redundant ones.
  • Treating "no formula" as "no concept needed." Concepts still anchor learning.

2. Approaches to Learning (ATL)

What stays: the five categories — Thinking, Communication, Social, Self-management, Research.

What changed:

  • The 10 sub-clusters are removed for PYP→MYP→DP coherence.
  • The "In order to… students must…" sentence is no longer required.
  • The terms implicit and explicit are gone — teachers decide when and how.
  • Skill development moves to the curriculum overview, not unit-by-unit logging.

Try this Monday: Write one sentence in plain English: "In this unit, students will practise [skill] by [task]." Done.

The 5 ATL categories — explore

Thinking

Critical, creative, transfer.

e.g. evaluate a source, generate alternative solutions, transfer a maths strategy to a science problem.
Communication

Exchanging information.

e.g. give and receive feedback, present a finding clearly, adapt tone for different audiences.
Social

Collaboration.

e.g. negotiate roles, resolve disagreement, share responsibility in group work.
Self-management

Organisation & affective.

e.g. plan deadlines, manage focus, persist after setbacks.
Research

Information & media literacy.

e.g. evaluate sources, organise notes, cross-reference data.

Old vs new ATL statement

Old: "In order to analyse the impact of trade routes, students must organise information from multiple sources (Research: information literacy)."

New: "Students will practise organising information from multiple sources (Research) as they investigate trade routes."

Plain-English definition

ATL skills are the transferable habits — how to think, talk, work together, manage yourself, and find things out — that make the content stick.

3. Global Contexts & Three Contextual Lenses

The six Global Contexts remain:

  1. Identities and relationships
  2. Orientation in space and time
  3. Personal and cultural expression
  4. Scientific and technical innovation
  5. Globalization and sustainability
  6. Fairness and development

What's new: three Contextual Lenses — Individual, Local, Global — used alongside (or instead of) a global context. The global context is optional in the inquiry framing.

Worked example — Statistics unit

Global Context: Fairness and Development.

Pick a lens above to see how the same unit can be reframed.

Why the lenses help

They fix the "compliance tag" problem — a fractions unit labelled Fairness and Development just because fractions can be used for fair sharing. The lenses force a real angle.

Pick one lens

You can combine, but don't tag all three. One genuine lens beats three vague ones.

4. Community Engagement

"Service as Action" becomes Community Engagement. The shift is from "for" to "with".

Four Learning Objectives

  1. Explore systems and develop awareness of your role.
  2. Foster dialogue and build relationships.
  3. Engage in reflective & reflexive practice.
  4. Develop and conduct principled action.

Five Forms of Engagement

Advocacy Action Research Social Entrepreneurship Participation Community-building

Try this Monday: Pick one current unit. Where could a student have an honest conversation with a community member that changes their thinking? That's your engagement angle.

From "to" to "with"

Old framing: "Year 8 will run a beach clean-up for the local town."

New framing: "Year 8 will meet with the local council and the waste team to co-design a behaviour-change campaign with them."

Plain-English definition

Community Engagement is what happens when students sit at the table with the people their learning affects — and then act, together.

Reference

Plain-English glossary

Filter the terms. Bookmark this page when you forget what something means.

Specified Concept
A big idea your subject keeps coming back to. Each subject has one curated list (~25+); schools may add their own. Replaces "Key" and "Related" concepts.
Inquiry Question
A provocative, non-Googleable question that frames a unit. Replaces the old Statement of Inquiry formula.
Inquiry Statement
The same role as the Inquiry Question, but phrased as a statement. Either is acceptable.
Statement of Inquiry (old)
One sentence combining a Key Concept + Related Concept + Global Context. No longer required.
ATL — Approaches to Learning
The transferable skills (Thinking, Communication, Social, Self-management, Research) students practise across every subject.
Skill cluster (old)
The 10 sub-categories under ATL. Removed in the Enhanced MYP.
Global Context
One of six big real-world frames (e.g. Fairness and Development) used to root learning in the wider world. Now optional in the inquiry framing.
Contextual Lens
Individual, Local, or Global. The specific angle from which a unit examines its context. Pick one — don't tag all three.
Community Engagement
The new name for "Service as Action". Action with the community, not to it. Four objectives, five forms.
Integrated Core
The four mandatory programme-wide elements: Interdisciplinary learning, ATL, Community Engagement, Self-Directed Projects.
Interdisciplinary Learning
Learning that deliberately combines two or more subject groups. At least one planned + assessed per year.
Community Project
A student-led project in Year 3. In the Enhanced MYP, no formal assessment criteria — evidence via journal, story, exhibition.
Personal Project
The capstone independent project in Year 5. Retained, with external moderation.
eAssessment
On-screen exams + ePortfolios for Year 5 students. First eAssessment under the new model is May 2030.
IB Exchange
The new interactive platform replacing static IB subject guide PDFs from 2027.
Subject-generic criteria
Shared rubric strands across subjects (Investigating, Applying, Evaluating). Suitable for younger MYP years.
Subject-specific criteria
Discipline-specific rubrics, used in later MYP years and required for Language Acquisition throughout.
Command term
An instruction verb like analyse, justify, investigate. Now embedded directly in achievement descriptors.
"I can…" statements
Asset-based, student-facing self-reflection statements aligned to the new criteria.

Common questions

FAQ — straight answers

When do I have to be teaching the Enhanced MYP?

September 2027. The beta is voluntary. The first eAssessment under the new model is May 2030 — schools currently in eAssessment shouldn't use beta guidance with those students until the 2027 official publication.

Do I have to throw out my old unit planners?

No. Re-plan one or two anchor units in the new framing this year. Iterate. Most existing content survives — it's the framing and the rubric that change.

Will my students still sit an MYP eAssessment?

Yes — same model (on-screen exam + ePortfolio), now aligned to the 3-criterion structure from May 2030.

Are Global Contexts dead?

No. They remain, but they're optional in the inquiry framing and now sit alongside the three Contextual Lenses (Individual / Local / Global).

What about the Personal Project?

Retained with external moderation. The Community Project (Y3) is simplified — no formal assessment criteria.

How do I know if a unit is "Enhanced MYP enough"?

Ask three things: (1) Could a 12-year-old answer my inquiry question without genuine thinking? If yes, rewrite it. (2) Is my Specified Concept doing real work in the assessment task? (3) Have I picked a specific Contextual Lens, not a generic tag?

I'm a new MYP teacher — where do I start?

Start with the Overview above, then plan one unit using the mini-planner. The four pillars are the headline — everything else is detail.
