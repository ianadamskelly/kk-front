What actually changes — in 90 seconds

The PYP didn't get a rewrite. It got two layers: the 2018 enhancement (Three Pillars, agency, action, play) is the live baseline; the 2025 evolution (subject continuums, ILPs, refreshed descriptors) is a fresh layer on top — mandatory by September 2027.

🧒

Learner first

Three pillars, in this order: the Learner, then Learning & Teaching, then the Learning Community. The child is named before the curriculum is.

🎙️

Agency, made concrete

Three surfaces: voice (the child is heard), choice (the child decides), ownership (the child takes responsibility). Every unit should show all three.

🔄

Action is a cycle

Choose → Act → Reflect. Action has five forms — participation, advocacy, social entrepreneurship, social justice, lifestyle choices — not just community service.

🎨

Play is learning

For ages 3–6, play is the primary mode of inquiry. Provoked by environment, documented by teachers, mapped to the central idea — not a warm-up to "real" learning.

📐

2025: Subject Continuums

Six new continuums replace the legacy Scope & Sequence. Progression by four developmental phases, not fixed year groups. Unified structure across all subjects.

🌉

Continuity to the MYP

The 2025 refresh and the 2027 MYP launch are deliberately the same date. Shared ATL, shared Learner Profile, and the Inquiry Learning Progressions span ages 5–16.

Read this if you're brand new to the PYP

The Primary Years Programme is the IB's framework for ages 3–12. Learning is transdisciplinary — the six themes (Who We Are, Where We Are in Place and Time, How We Express Ourselves, How the World Works, How We Organize Ourselves, Sharing the Planet) frame inquiry across subjects. Each unit of inquiry is anchored by a Central Idea and 3–4 Lines of Inquiry. Children build a portfolio, run a student-led conference each year, and finish with the PYP Exhibition (PYPX) in their final year.

The signature

The six transdisciplinary themes

The PYP's defining structural feature. Six themes that, together, capture what is worth inquiring into across the primary years. Each is taught once per year-group (the PYPX year explores five through teacher-designed UOIs and one through the children's exhibition). The 2025 evolution refreshed each descriptor — a single driving statement plus three conceptual supports.

🪞

Who We Are

An inquiry into the nature of the self, who we are as individuals, and who we are together.

  • Identity, beliefs and values shape how we see ourselves and others.
  • Physical, mental, social and spiritual well-being are connected.
  • Families, friendships, communities and cultures hold us together.
🧭

Where We Are in Place and Time

An inquiry into orientation in place and time, our shared histories, and our connection to the natural world.

  • People, places and ideas move across time — and leave traces.
  • The natural world has its own histories that overlap with ours.
  • Discovery, exploration and migration shape everyone they touch.
🎭

How We Express Ourselves

An inquiry into the ways we discover and express ideas, feelings, nature, culture and beliefs — and the aesthetic.

  • Creativity is how humans share what cannot easily be said.
  • Audiences shape what is made, just as makers shape audiences.
  • Expression carries the values and questions of its maker.
🔬

How the World Works

An inquiry into the natural world and its laws, and into the impact of scientific and technological advances on humans and the environment.

  • Living and non-living systems behave in patterns we can study.
  • Technologies extend what humans can do — and change us in return.
  • Every transformation in nature or technology has costs and consequences.
🏛️

How We Organize Ourselves

An inquiry into the interconnectedness of human-made systems and communities — structures, organisations, decision-making and economic activity.

  • Communities organise themselves to meet shared needs.
  • Systems shape what is produced, exchanged and valued.
  • Decisions made by a few often affect many.
🌍

Sharing the Planet

An inquiry into rights and responsibilities in the struggle to share finite resources with other people and other living things — peace and conflict resolution.

  • All living things depend on one another and on the planet.
  • Resources are finite, and how they are shared matters.
  • Rights and responsibilities exist together; neither survives alone.
What changed in 2025 — and what didn't

The six theme names are unchanged. The descriptors were refreshed: each opens with a single driving statement followed by three concise conceptual supports (down from the older, denser paragraphs). The new descriptors deliberately rebalance human commonalities with the natural world — a small but significant philosophical shift acknowledging that the PYP child lives in an ecosystem, not just a society.

Deep dive

The three pillars (+ the 2025 evolution)

Tap a tab to explore each pillar. Each section has a "What changed", "Why it matters", and a "Try this Monday" prompt.

Pillar 1 — The Learner

The pillar named first, on purpose. The PYP starts from the child, not the curriculum document. This pillar holds the Learner Profile, student agency, the action cycle, and learner well-being.

Agency — three concrete surfaces

🎙️VoiceThe learner is heard. "What did you notice? What do you wonder?"
🤲ChoiceThe learner decides. Which line of inquiry? Which artefact? Which audience?
📔OwnershipThe learner takes responsibility. Co-construct criteria, run the conference, curate the portfolio.

The Action Cycle

The 2018 framework replaced the old "Service Learning" framing with a continuous cycle: Choose → Act → Reflect. Action has five forms, not just community service.

Participation Advocacy Social entrepreneurship Social justice Lifestyle choices

The 10 Learner Profile attributes

InquirersKnowledgeableThinkersCommunicators PrincipledOpen-mindedCaringRisk-takers BalancedReflective

Try this Monday: Pick one current unit. Find one place each for voice, choice, and ownership. If you can't find all three, you've found your next iteration.

Plain-English definition

Student agency is the difference between "I'm being taught" and "I'm doing the learning." Voice = heard. Choice = decides. Ownership = responsible.

Watch out for…

  • "Pick blue or red" framed as agency. (Allocation, not choice.)
  • Action that is all charity and no relationship.
  • Treating the early years as a warm-up. Play is the inquiry.

Pillar 2 — Learning and Teaching

This pillar holds the curriculum-shaped machinery: the six transdisciplinary themes, the seven key concepts, the Approaches to Learning, and the Central Idea + Lines of Inquiry framing of every unit.

The 7 Key Concepts

Form
What is it like?
Function
How does it work?
Causation
Why is it like it is?
Change
How is it transforming?
Connection
How is it linked to other things?
Perspective
What are the points of view?
Responsibility
What is our obligation?

Reflection (the old 8th concept) was removed in 2018 — it is now a programme-wide practice rather than a unit-bound concept.

The 5 ATL categories

Thinking

Notice, wonder, question.

e.g. compare two stories, predict what will happen, infer a character's motive.
Communication

Listen, speak, read, write, show.

e.g. present a finding, listen to feedback, draw a diagram.
Social

Cooperate, share, resolve.

e.g. take turns, negotiate roles, include someone left out.
Self-management

Plan, organise, persist.

e.g. set a goal, manage time, try again after a setback.
Research

Observe, find out, cite.

e.g. interview a parent, look up a source, record observations.

Central Idea + Lines of Inquiry — worked example

Theme: Sharing the Planet · Phase 3 (Year 3)

Central Idea: Freshwater is a finite resource that sustains all life and depends on the choices people make.

  1. Where freshwater comes from and where it goes form
  2. How human activity affects freshwater systems causation
  3. The responsibilities of individuals and communities towards shared water responsibility

Try this Monday: Re-write one current Central Idea. Strip any proper nouns and dates. Add a concept-verb (depends on, shapes, sustains, influences). Test it: would the idea still hold if you taught the unit in a different country?

Plain-English definition

A Central Idea is a single concept-rich sentence stating the enduring understanding the unit is built around — not a topic, not a question, not an opinion.

A Line of Inquiry is a focused conceptual angle on the Central Idea — usually a noun phrase, 5–12 words.

Watch out for…

  • A Central Idea that is really a topic ("Water").
  • A Central Idea written as a question.
  • Five or six Lines of Inquiry. (3–4 is the sweet spot.)

Pillar 3 — The Learning Community

The pillar that puts everyone else in the frame: collaborative planning teams, families, partnerships beyond the school, and the wider IB community. The unit of work in this pillar is the relationship, not the lesson.

What "Learning Community" actually means

  • Collaborative planning — teachers plan UOIs together, not in isolation. The planner is a thinking tool, not a compliance document.
  • Families as partners — student-led conferences, three-way conferences, portfolios shared with families, mother-tongue support.
  • Beyond the school — local experts, alumni, council, NGOs, the local environment as collaborators in action.
  • The IB community — connections with other IB schools (PYP, MYP, DP) for continuity and shared inquiry.

The PYP Exhibition (PYPX)

The PYPX is where all three pillars meet. In the final year of the PYP, students choose a transdisciplinary theme, form groups of ≤ 5, work with a mentor, drive their own inquiry, and culminate in a public exhibition of learning and a real-world action.

  • 6–8 weeks, student-led, collaborative.
  • Mentors as facilitators, never as researchers.
  • Central Idea and Lines of Inquiry authored by the students.
  • Public sharing — to families, the school, sometimes the wider community.

Try this Monday: Host one student-led conference even if your school normally does three-ways. Reflect on how it felt to be the quietest adult in the room.

Plain-English definition

The learning community is everyone who has a stake in this child's learning — teachers, families, peers, neighbours, mentors, the planet. Education is collective.

The PYPX in one line

The IB's clearest expression of what a PYP graduate looks like: a curious, collaborative, principled child who can drive their own inquiry, work in a team, and act on what they have learned.

★ The 2025 evolution — mandatory by Sept 2027

In April 2025 the IB published a fresh layer on top of the 2018 framework. The themes, pillars and concepts are unchanged. What's new is the infrastructure around them.

1 · Refreshed transdisciplinary descriptors

Each of the six themes now opens with a single driving statement followed by three conceptual supports — down from longer, denser paragraphs. The new descriptors deliberately rebalance human commonalities with the natural world.

2 · Six new Subject Continuums

Replacing the legacy Scope & Sequence documents. All six follow a unified structure:

  1. Overall Expectations — the big picture by phase.
  2. Conceptual Understandings — the durable ideas.
  3. Learning Outcomes — clearer, schools can map to local standards.

3 · Four developmental phases

Progression by phase, not by year group — respecting the wide developmental range in any primary classroom.

Phase 1ages 3–5

Sensory, playful, here-and-now. Inquiry through provocation and shared wonder.

Phase 2ages 5–7

Concrete, story-driven. Inquiry through observation, classification, role-play.

Phase 3ages 7–9

Pattern-seeking, beginning to generalise. Inquiry through investigation and structured action.

Phase 4ages 9–12

Abstract, multi-perspective, student-led. Inquiry through research, debate, authored action (PYPX).

4 · Inquiry Learning Progressions (ILPs)

A new document describing how inquiry skills — observing, questioning, role-taking, decision-making, hypothesising — develop in sophistication across ages 5–16. The first IB document to formally span PYP into MYP Years 1–2 — the vertical spine of PYP→MYP continuity.

Try this Monday: Read the new Subject Continuum for your strongest subject. Sketch how the four developmental phases map onto the year groups you teach.

Why this matters

The 2025 deadline (September 2027) deliberately aligns with the Enhanced MYP launch. PYP and MYP teachers will, for the first time, share a published vertical document. The cliff between Year 6 and MYP Year 1 narrows.

What hasn't changed

  • The three pillars.
  • The six theme names.
  • The seven key concepts.
  • The five ATL categories.
  • The 10 Learner Profile attributes.

Reference

Plain-English glossary

Filter the terms. Bookmark this page when you forget what something means.

Central Idea
A single, concept-rich statement of the enduring understanding the unit is built around. Not a topic, not a question, not an opinion.
Line of Inquiry
A focused conceptual angle on the Central Idea — usually a noun phrase, 5–12 words. Each UOI typically has 3–4.
Programme of Inquiry (POI)
The school-wide map of every Unit of Inquiry, organised by year-group across the six transdisciplinary themes. A living document.
Unit of Inquiry (UOI)
Typically 6–8 weeks of transdisciplinary learning anchored by a Central Idea, 3–4 Lines of Inquiry, key concepts, ATL skills, Learner Profile attributes, and anticipated action.
Transdisciplinary theme
One of six themes (Who We Are; Where We Are in Place and Time; How We Express Ourselves; How the World Works; How We Organize Ourselves; Sharing the Planet) that frame inquiry across subjects.
Key Concept
One of the seven transdisciplinary concepts: form, function, causation, change, connection, perspective, responsibility. Reflection was removed in 2018.
Related / Additional Concept
Subject-specific concepts (e.g. pattern in mathematics, voice in language). Drawn from the 2025 Subject Continuums.
ATL — Approaches to Learning
Five categories — Thinking, Communication, Social, Self-management, Research — shared across PYP, MYP and DP.
Learner Profile
The 10 IB attributes (Inquirers, Knowledgeable, Thinkers, Communicators, Principled, Open-minded, Caring, Risk-takers, Balanced, Reflective). Now also carry what used to be called "the 12 attitudes".
Agency
Voice (heard), choice (decides), ownership (responsible). The 2018 framework's central organising principle.
Action Cycle
Choose → Act → Reflect. Action has five forms — participation, advocacy, social entrepreneurship, social justice, lifestyle choices.
PYP Exhibition (PYPX)
The capstone in the final PYP year. 6–8 weeks, student-led, collaborative, transdisciplinary inquiry with a real-world action and a public sharing.
Subject Continuum
The 2025 replacement for the legacy Scope & Sequence. Six continuums, one per subject, unified structure (Overall Expectations → Conceptual Understandings → Learning Outcomes), phase-based.
Developmental Phases
The four 2025 progression bands — Phase 1 (3–5), Phase 2 (5–7), Phase 3 (7–9), Phase 4 (9–12) — that replace fixed grade-level standards.
Inquiry Learning Progressions (ILPs)
The 2025 document describing how inquiry skills mature across ages 5–16, spanning PYP into MYP Years 1–2. The first published PYP→MYP vertical spine.
Learning through play
For ages 3–6, the 2018 framework explicitly names play as the primary mode of inquiry — purposeful, documented, child-led, mapped to the Central Idea.
Student-led conference
A meeting the child runs with their family, walking them through their own portfolio and goals. The teacher is present but quiet.
Three pillars
The 2018 framework's structure: the Learner; Learning and Teaching; the Learning Community. The order matters — the child is named first.
Five essential elements (old)
Pre-2018: knowledge, concepts, skills, attitudes, action. Replaced in 2018 by the three pillars.
Transdisciplinary (vs cross-curricular)
Cross-curricular = subjects clustered around a topic. Transdisciplinary = subjects disappear into the inquiry. The PYP is the latter.

Common questions

FAQ — straight answers

When do I have to be using the 2025 updates?

September 2027. That is the IB's mandatory full-implementation deadline for the new Subject Continuums and the refreshed descriptors — deliberately aligned with the Enhanced MYP launch. The 2018 framework remains the underlying baseline; 2025 is a layer on top.

Do I have to throw out my current POI?

No. Audit it against the 2025 transdisciplinary descriptors — is each unit still aligned with the refreshed "natural world balance" framing? Re-write one or two anchor Central Ideas. Iterate.

Is there a PYP eAssessment coming?

No. The PYP remains exam-free; periodic rumours of a PYP eAssessment are unfounded as of 2026. Assessment is formative, portfolio-led, conference-based.

What happened to "Service Learning"?

It was replaced in 2018 by the Action Cycle (Choose → Act → Reflect). Action has five forms — community service is one. Lifestyle choices and advocacy count.

What about the 12 "PYP attitudes"?

Folded into the 10 Learner Profile attributes in 2018. Schools still teaching the separate attitudes list are running on the pre-2018 framework.

How do I know if a Central Idea is "good enough"?

Three tests: (1) Is it transferable? Strip the proper nouns and dates — does the idea still hold? (2) Does it use a concept-verb (depends on / shapes / sustains / influences)? (3) Could you teach the same Central Idea in a different country and have it still mean something? If all three: ship it.

Is the PYPX changing?

No formal IB signal that the PYPX format is changing. Some school-led conversation about lighter-touch options (mini-exhibitions in earlier years) is around — but it's school-driven, not IB-driven.

I'm a new PYP teacher — where do I start?

Start with the Overview, then the six themes, then plan one UOI. The three pillars and the Central Idea + Lines of Inquiry framing are the headline — everything else is detail.
